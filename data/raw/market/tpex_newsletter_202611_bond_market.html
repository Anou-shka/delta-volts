Bond Market


In the U.S. bond market, the U.S. Manufacturing Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) for September, reported on October 1 by the Institute for Supply Management (ISM), dropped to 54.5, lower than that of the preceding month by 0.1 and the market expectation of 55.0. According to the September non-farm payroll and unemployment statistics released by the Bureau of Labor Statistics on October 2, non-farm jobs rose by 29,000, lower than the market expectation of 90,000 new jobs; the number of new jobs in August was revised downward from 162,000 jobs to 133,000 jobs; the unemployment rate in September rose to 4.2%, higher than market expectations and that of the preceding month of 4.1%. The September Consumer Price Index (CPI) published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics on October 14 increased to 3.7% from 3.4% in August, which was higher than market expectations of 3.6%. The annual core CPI growth rate for September rose to 2.6% from 2.4% in August, higher than market expectations of 2.5%. On October 28, the Federal Reserve (Fed) voted 10-2 to maintain the federal funds rate in the range of 3.75% to 4.00%, in line with market expectations. The statement adjusted the wording related to economic and inflation developments, changing the characterization of economic activity from “a solid pace” to “a strong pace,” and emphasizing that “the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal.” At the press conference following the meeting, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh stated that although the progress in curbing inflation since the September rate hike was encouraging, he acknowledged that the Fed has not yet achieved price stability and that inflation remains too high, emphasizing that the 2% target is not to be compromised. He explicitly opposed forward guidance, the practice of signaling policy decisions in advance, but was unwilling to predict the outcome of the next meeting, only assuring that the Fed would continue to provide new monetary remedies to curb inflation. U.S. Treasury yields fluctuated and declined in October, mainly due to increased selling pressure on AI-related technology stocks, which prompted investors to turn to safe-haven assets such as government bonds, leading to a decline in U.S. Treasury yields. As of October 31, the yield on the 10-year U.S. Treasury note closed at 4.929%, down 35.6 basis points from the level at the end of September (5.285%).

In the European bond market, market research firm S&P Global reported on October 1 that the final value of Eurozone manufacturing PMI for September was 52.7. Eurostat released its final Eurozone CPI annual growth rate for September of 3.8% on October 16, which was higher than that (3.2%) of the month earlier. The final reading for the core CPI annual growth rate was 2.5%, higher than the previous month's 2.4%. The European Central Bank (ECB) held its monetary policy meeting on October 29, concluding that the three key interest rates should not be adjusted for now. The ECB noted that the Middle East conflict has pushed up energy prices, tilting inflation risks to the upside and economic growth risks to the downside. Although core inflation and wages have not yet shown a significant acceleration, the duration and magnitude of energy price increases may further drive inflation through indirect and second-round effects. At a press conference following the meeting, ECB President Christine Lagarde stated that the ECB had discussed the necessity of raising interest rates, but considering that core inflation remained largely stable, it decided to maintain the current interest rate for the time being. The ECB will use the latest data on inflation, wages, and economic growth obtained before the December meeting to assess the direction of subsequent policies. European government bond yields diverged in October. In Germany, increased uncertainty surrounding French fiscal and political developments led to a shift of funds towards relatively safe assets such as German government bonds. This, coupled with selling pressure on global technology stocks triggering a demand for safe-haven assets, drove the 10-year government bond yield down 26.4 basis points to 3.322%. In France, the government's heavy fiscal deficit and debt burden, coupled with increased political uncertainty ahead of the 2027 presidential election, have deepened market doubts about its capacity for fiscal consolidation, pushing up the risk premium for government bonds. The yield on 10-year government bonds rose by 11.5 basis points to 4.972%. In Italy, the yield on 10-year government bonds fell 10 basis points to 4.513%, showing relative stability.

Domestically, the September Taiwanese monitoring indicators published by the National Development Council on October 29 showed that the latest economic composite score was 41, one point higher than the preceding month (40), while the economic monitoring indicator continued to flash red. According to the import/export data published by the Ministry of Finance on October 9, the exports in September reached a record high over any given month in the past of US$87.22 billion, up 60.9% compared to that of the same period last year (an increase of 68.2% in NTD terms). In October, Taiwan government bond yields fluctuated upward. At the beginning of the month, the market was affected by selling pressure on short-term government bonds. In addition, the Consumer Price Index (CPI) released by Taiwan was higher than market expectations, which further strengthened market expectations that the Central Bank might raise interest rates in December, driving up the yields of government bonds of all maturities. In addition, the recent large issuance of corporate bonds has partially displaced the demand for government bond investment. The yield on 10-year Taiwanese government bonds as of October 31 closed at 2.0300%, up 10.95 bps from the end of the previous month.

The 5-year and 10-year benchmark government bond yields closed at 1.85% and 2.03%, respectively, as of October 31 (as compared to 1.86% and 1.92% at the end of September). The daily turnover in the NTD bond market averaged NT$148.288 billion for the month of October 2026. The daily average of outright trade amounted to NT$8.940 billion (6.03%), and that of RP/RS trade was NT$139.349 billion (93.73%). The daily average of market turnover increased 6.54% as compared to that in September (the daily market turnover in the month of September averaged NT$139.187 billion; it amounted to NT$8.861 billion (6.37%) for outright trade and NT$130.326 billion (93.63%) for RP/RS trade). In the foreign-currency bond market, the daily turnover of international bonds and registered foreign bonds averaged NT$34.806 billion for the month of October 2026; it amounted to NT$6.705 billion (19.26%) for outright trade and NT$28.101 billion (80.74%) for RP/RS trade. The daily average market turnover decreased 41.56% as compared to that of September 2026 (while the daily market turnover in the month of September averaged NT$59.556 billion; it amounted to NT$9.301 billion (15.62%) for outright trade and NT$50.255 billion (84.38%) for RP/RS trade).


Major stock exchanges in the world, such as Stockholm Stock Exchange, London Stock Exchange, Luxembourg Stock Exchange, Euronext, Korea Exchange, Hong Kong Exchange, and Singapore Exchange have set up a special board for the trading of sustainable bond, economic, social, and governance (ESG) bond or socially responsible investment (SRI) bond. In a move to promote Taiwan's sustainable finance, continue to keep abreast of international markets and enhance the international visibility of our capital market, and in support of the Corporate Governance 3.0 – Sustainable Development Roadmap, Green Finance Action Plan 3.0 as well as Capital Market Roadmap 2021–2023 promoted by the competent authorities, Taipei Exchange (TPEx), in reference of international trends, integrated the green bond listing and trading mechanism, social bond listing and trading mechanism, and sustainability bond listing and trading mechanism into the sustainable bond listing and trading mechanism and has set up a Sustainable Bond Board. On top of the capital bonds dedicated to sustainable developments, in order to assist enterprises achieve their overall strategic sustainable development goals, work toward net zero emissions and sustainable transformations, as well as to expand the range of sustainable bonds offered in Taiwan, the TPEx has promulgated the Sustainability-Linked Bond (SLB) trading system on July 8, 2022 so as to offer even more diversified sustainable fund-raising and investment tools, as well as to keep abreast of international markets for issuers and investors at home and abroad.

Furthermore, to provide the market with well-rounded information and contents on sustainable bonds, the TPEx has formulated a dedicated Sustainable Bond website (https://www.tpex.org.tw/web/bond/sustainability/index.php?l=zh-tw). On top of relevant information such as Taiwan's progress in developing sustainable bonds over the years, summaries on the bonds, overviews on bond issuance (e.g., issuing criteria, issuance plan and post-market report), latest information, and research reports, the website also aims to actively strengthen ESG information disclosure on the bond issuers. "Issuer's Sustainable Development Strategies" and "Sustainable Bond Benefits" sections have been added to the website since 2022. The convenient and user-friendly interface is designed to provide market participants with comprehensive and enriched reference information, thereby allowing them to understand the latest development trends and directions in the sustainable bond market both at home and abroad.
