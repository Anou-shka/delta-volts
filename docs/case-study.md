# Case study: Delta Electronics (2308.TW) — the AI power play

*Equity research initiation · built October–November 2026 · [GitHub repo](https://github.com/Anou-shka/delta-volts)*

> Draft for my website `[ANALYST TO REVIEW]`. Numbers are as of 23 Nov 2026 and use the draft Base case.

## The question

Delta Electronics makes the power supplies, power distribution and liquid cooling that go into AI server racks. Revenue grew 32% in 2025 and 37% in the first nine months of 2026, and management says AI data-centre businesses are now about **half of revenue**. Then the stock more than halved in seven weeks, from NT$2,050 (6 Oct) to **NT$990** (23 Nov), in an AI-led sell-off across Taiwan. I wanted to answer: **how much of Delta is an AI bet, and what is the market still paying for?**

## What I built

- **A primary-source data pipeline.** 42 filings, slides and transcripts from Delta's investor site are parsed by code into five years of financial statements. **440 automated checks** prove the extraction ties to every reported subtotal, and each of 1,000+ figures is traced to a document and page.
- **A three-statement model in Excel, generated by code.** It has about 2,600 live formulas, a segment-driven revenue build with an explicit AI data-centre line, and Bear/Base/Bull scenarios to FY2030. The balance sheet balances in every year and scenario.
- **A valuation.** It covers WACC (2.03% Taiwan 10-year, Damodaran ERP and country risk, beta 1.48), a DCF, peer comps, sensitivities, a football field and a **reverse DCF**.
- **A Python mirror of the model.** It recomputes every forecast and valuation number; a test fails if it ever disagrees with the spreadsheet by more than 0.1%.
- **A 10-page initiation report generated from the model**, so the text can't drift from the numbers.

## What I found

- **The AI exposure is real and audited.** Infrastructure profit rose from NT$5.5bn to NT$28.4bn in 2025, and group operating margin went from 10% (2021) to 17% (9M26).
- **The price still assumes a lot.** On my Base case the DCF is worth NT$675. To justify NT$990, AI data-centre capex would need to grow about **48% a year in 2027–30**, against 14% in my Base case, or the market is paying for growth well beyond 2030.
- **Tariff risk is structural.** The US is 36% of revenue but holds 3% of Delta's long-term assets; production sits in Thailand, Taiwan and China.

## Judgement calls I'm proud of

- **I refused an unsourced number.** The widely quoted "AI power 20%, cooling 9%" split isn't in any Delta document, so the AI line is anchored only on management's own statements. When the CFO corrected the AI figures on the Q3 call, the model followed.
- **I made the biggest judgement visible.** Terminal growth capped at the NT$ risk-free rate gives NT$675 a share; a peer-style 12× exit multiple gives NT$1,010. The report shows both, because that gap is the investment debate.
- **I wrote every decision down.** 18 dated decisions, each with its reasoning and its effect on the model.
